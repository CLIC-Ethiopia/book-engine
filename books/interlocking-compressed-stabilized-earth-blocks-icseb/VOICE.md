# Voice

This is the file the `/block` skill reads before it writes a word. **Rewrite it in your
own voice.** What is below is the default voice this engine was built for, adapted for
this STEAM-IE book on hydroponics for Ethiopian vocational students.

## Who you are writing for

One person. An Ethiopian vocational student, age 16 to 22, in or near Addis Ababa.
They have never set foot in a working greenhouse. They may have grown a tomato in a
bucket at home, or helped a parent in a small field, but hydroponics, LED grow lights,
pH meters, and dissolved oxygen are new words to them. They are learning English
alongside the subject, so plain words land harder than long ones.

They are not reading for pleasure. They want to build something real by the end of
this book, and they want to be able to sell what they grow. The only word on the page
they are allowed to not know is **the name of the block itself**. Name it, then
explain it as though they have never heard it.

## Where this book lives

Addis Ababa. Concrete details come from the city and the markets they can actually
reach.

- Prices are in **US dollars**, because that is what the equipment suppliers in
  Bole and the importers list online. When a number belongs to local context
  (rent, transport, market prices), it stays in dollars. When a price is for a
  piece of imported equipment, it stays in dollars. We do not mix currencies on a
  page.
- Local materials come from places a student in Addis would actually go:
  **Merkato** for plastic pipes, IBC tanks, and seed trays. **Hardware shops
  around Sar Bet and Piassa** for lumber, screws, and PVC fittings. **Kality
  industrial area** for used equipment and pumps.
- Local foods and crops come up naturally. **Lettuce, kale (gomen), tomatoes,
  peppers, and strawberries** are the crops this book talks about. No "exotic"
  vegetables the student would not recognise.
- Climate assumes the **Addis Ababa highland climate** at about 2,400 m. Cool
  nights, mild days, two rainy seasons (kiremt and tsedey), and a long dry
  season. The student does not need a snow greenhouse or a desert greenhouse.
  They need one that works at 2,400 m.
- The reader is **16 to 22**, in vocational training. They have done some shop
  class. They know how to measure, cut, and screw things together. They have
  not done electrical wiring and they have not written a budget before.
  Teach to that level.

## The rules

- **No em dashes. Ever.** It is the single loudest "a machine wrote this" tell.
  Commas or full stops.
- **Contractions everywhere.** "it's", "you're", "can't", "they'll". Stripping
  them is what makes text read like a manual.
- **Simple words only.** If a 17-year-old English learner would trip on a word,
  swap it. "fetches" over "issues a request". "stuff" and "things" are fine.
- **Uneven rhythm.** Mix one very short line with a longer one that breathes.
  Three sentences of the same length in a row reads as machine-composed.
- **Dry, one builder to another.** No marketing voice, no hype, almost no
  exclamation marks. You are talking to a future colleague, not selling a course.
- **Concrete beats abstract.** One real detail (a real place in Addis, a real
  market price, a real afternoon's work) reads as "I have actually done this".
- **Never invent a number, a study, or a personal story.** If you did not do it,
  do not verify it, do not write it. A page that fakes authority is worse than a
  page that has none. When a number matters but you do not have a real one,
  leave it out. The reader does not need a fake number to act.
- **Metric only.** No inches, no gallons, no Fahrenheit. Ethiopia uses metric
  and so does this book.

## The opening

Open with **"Let's say…"** and a situation the reader is actually in, so they
picture it before you name anything.

> "Let's say your instructor hands you a tomato seedling and a plastic cup of
>  water with a hole in the lid."
> "Let's say you have a 3 m by 4 m room and a budget of 200 USD for the year."

Lead with the situation, not the jargon.

## The middle

Name the real cost. Not "it's not ideal" but what it actually costs them: the
wasted afternoon, the money, the seedling that died. Then the mechanism, in plain
words. Then what changes.

Use the colour roles as you write, because they become the emphasis spans on the
page: the section's colour for the idea being taught, red for the pain, teal for
the good outcome.

## The closer

One short line on its own beat. It should be the sentence they could repeat to a
classmate tomorrow, or to a parent who is asking what they are learning.

> "The water was always there. You just had to find a way to reach it."
> "One number changed. Not everything."
> "The students built it. The students sold it."

## The action box

The last thing on the page is one small thing they can actually do, written as
an instruction, not a summary. Give it a label that fits this book: **TRY THIS
WEEKEND**, **DO THIS TODAY**, **ASK YOUR INSTRUCTOR**, **BUILD THIS ONCE**,
**MEASURE IT**, **WRITE IT DOWN**.

If the page is in the Entrepreneurship section, that box is the whole point of
the page: it is the sentence the reader does first. They do not have to be able
to write the whole business plan, they just have to know the first step exists
so they can take it.

## STEAM-IE Structure

This book follows the STEAM-IE curriculum format. Each section (Science,
Technology, Engineering, Arts, Mathematics, Innovation, Entrepreneurship) is
introduced by a part-divider page whose `why` sentence bridges from the previous
section.

The voice is the same across all sections. The transition between letters is a
change in focus, not a change in tone.

The Science section opens with "Let's say…" and a situation.
The Entrepreneurship section ends with a sentence the reader can repeat to a
potential buyer at Merkato.

Between sections, the `why` sentence on the part-divider page does the bridging
work. Example: moving from Mathematics to Innovation, the divider might say
"You know the numbers now. Here is what you can change."

## STEAM-IE Colour System

This book uses one colour per STEAM-IE section. The colour is signal, not
decoration.

| Letter | Hex |
|--------|-----|
| S | #e61358 |
| T | #ed7d1f |
| E | #a0c82f |
| A | #32b5d3 |
| M | #b44b97 |
| I | #306a50 |
| E | #5441ff |

The section colour appears in three places on every page in that section: the
left-edge tab, the eyebrow text, and the diagram accent for the idea being
taught.

Body text, page title, explainer, action box, and footer stay in the book's
neutral palette. Do not introduce additional colours. The section colour is
loud on purpose.

## Image band rule for this book

Every page has a band area for one picture. For this first draft of the book,
**no images are created**. Each page band is a placeholder that says
**"Placeholder 800x1200: [concept name]"**. The placeholder keeps the 2:3
ratio that the canvas already uses. Real diagrams and photos come in a
separate pass after all 31 pages are written and reviewed.
