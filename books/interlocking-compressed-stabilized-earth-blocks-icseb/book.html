Soil Composition Basics: Balancing sand, silt, and clay for mechanical stabilization

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 01
Science

Soil Composition Basics

Balancing sand, silt, and clay for mechanical stabilization

Let's say your mix fails and you reach for more cement. Cement is not what holds soil together.

Interlocking blocks rely on particle distribution, with clay acting as a natural binder and sand as the structural skeleton. Ideal soil is 50 to 70% sand, 15 to 30% silt, and 10 to 20% clay. Test the profile at the quarry before mixing, because too much clay swells and cracks while too little crumbles.

Read the soil before you read the cement bag.

TRY THIS TODAY

Perform a simple Jar Test: fill a straight-sided glass jar 1/3 full with dry field soil, add water and 1 tsp salt, shake vigorously for 2 minutes, and measure settling layers at 1 minute (sand), 2 hours (silt), and 48 hours (clay).

Hydration Mechanics: How OPC cement bonds soil particles under compaction

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 02
Science

Hydration Mechanics

How OPC cement bonds soil particles under compaction

Let's say your soil-cement block is weak and crumbly. Water content is usually the culprit.

Ordinary Portland Cement stabilizes soil through hydration, and calcium silicate hydrate gels lock the grains together. Compaction moisture must sit at the optimum, typically 8 to 12% by weight. Too dry and hydration fails, leaving air pockets. Too wet and water absorbs the compaction energy.

Get the moisture right and cheap earth becomes weather-resistant masonry.

DO THIS TODAY

Squeeze moist soil-cement into a ball and drop it from 1 m. Four or five lumps means moisture is optimal.

Curing Chemistry: Managing moisture retention for full strength gain

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 03
Science

Curing Chemistry

Managing moisture retention for full strength gain

Let's say your blocks look strong at a week, then fail at a month. They never hardened, they only dried.

ICSEB blocks gain strength through a moisture-dependent chemical reaction. Premature drying stops cement hydration and costs up to 60% of compressive strength. Hold near 100% humidity for 7 days for initial strength and 28 days for full design strength.

Water and sheeting do the curing. Heat alone does not.

Set up a dedicated curing pad using thick 200-micron black polythene sheeting on a flat shaded surface, elevated on wooden pallets to prevent ground water suction.

Compaction Physics: Denser soil matrices resist moisture and shear stress

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 04
Science

Compaction Physics

Denser soil matrices resist moisture and shear stress

Let's say your blocks crumble the moment they get wet. Uncompressed earth has no strength to resist soaking.

Compaction forces residual air out and lifts bulk density to 1,800 to 2,000 kg/m³, letting particles bond electrostatically and chemically. That dense matrix sheds water and survives severe weather cycles.

Pressure is what turns loose soil into a wall.

MEASURE IT

Weigh a newly pressed block immediately after compaction and divide by its volume to calculate green wet density; target at least 1.95 g/cm³.

Clay Plasticity Index: Measuring swelling potential with Atterberg Limits

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 05
Science

Clay Plasticity Index

Measuring swelling potential with Atterberg Limits

Let's say your finished wall cracks as it dries. The clay in your soil moved too much.

The Plasticity Index measures how much soil changes volume with water. Clays above PI 25 swell when wet and shrink when dry, cracking structure. Soils at PI 8 to 15 suit ICSEB. Blend high-clay soils with sharp pit sand before adding stabilizers.

Know your PI before you buy the clay.

TRY THIS TODAY

Perform the Ribbon Test: roll moist soil into a 3mm thread between your palms; if it forms a ribbon over 5cm before breaking, blend in 30% sand to lower plasticity.

Erosion & Water Action: Capillary action, splash degradation, and waterproofing

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 06
Science

Erosion & Water Action

Capillary action, splash degradation, and waterproofing

Let's say rain is washing your wall face away. Bare earth blocks drink water and give it back as pressure.

Water attacks two ways: raindrop erosion and capillary rise from the ground. Cement coating of clay minerals cuts water absorption below 15%. Silicone admixtures and sealers break surface tension, so rain beads and rolls off without trapping vapour inside.

Keep water outside the wall, not inside it.

TRY THIS WEEKEND

Place a cured block in a shallow pan with 1cm of water for 4 hours; observe how far moisture creeps up the sides to verify capillary resistance.

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 07
Science

Thermal Mass Physics

Passive cooling and heat storage capacity in earth walls

Let's say your rooms swing hot and cold all day. Thin walls pass that swing straight through.

Compressed earth carries high thermal mass, about 0.85 kJ/kg·K. It soaks up outdoor heat by day and radiates it inward at night, damping indoor swings by up to 8°C.

A heavy wall is free air conditioning.

MEASURE IT

Measure wall surface temperatures inside and outside an earth building at 2:00 PM and 2:00 AM using an infrared thermometer to verify thermal lag.

Preventing mix failures with digital moisture testing

Mold Liner Maintenance: Hardened steel plates and anti-stick coatings

STEAM-IE for Eco-Construction · No. 13

Mold Liner Maintenance

Hardened steel plates and anti-stick coatings

Let's say your mold walls wear out fast. Silica sand is eating your steel.

Press molds take severe abrasion from silica. Hard-faced tool steel liners such as HARDOX 400 or chrome-plated steel extend mold life beyond 100,000 presses. Spraying interiors with a 10:1 diesel and recycled motor oil mix keeps soil from sticking and block edges crisp.

The liner decides how many blocks you get per mold.

MEASURE IT

Inspect internal mold box dimensions with a digital micrometer every 5,000 blocks; replace wear plates when abrasion exceeds 0.8mm variance.
